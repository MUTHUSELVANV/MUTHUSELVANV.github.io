October 2025

Selected as Exemplary Project – IOE Prospective Student Workshop 2025

University of Michigan, Ann Arbor

Our IOE 591 (W25) project team was selected to present our research at the 2025 IOE Prospective Student Workshop, where exemplary projects were showcased to prospective graduate students, faculty, and staff. The team consisted of myself, Sanjay Kandaswamy, and Yuhan Jiang. The event brought together around 60 to 65 prospective students exploring the IOE program, and our role was to walk them through the project end to end, explaining the process, the concepts we applied, and the reasoning behind our approach. A good part of the challenge was translating technical concepts like discrete-event simulation, statistical distributions, and predictive modeling into language a non-technical audience could follow and engage with.

Beyond the project itself, students had a lot of follow-up questions about the course, other IOE electives, and how coursework connected to real work. Many were also curious about future career paths, internships, and university life at Michigan. We spent a good part of the session answering these questions and sharing our own experience navigating the program.

I connected with several students on LinkedIn afterwards and stayed in touch. Around 4 to 5 of them are still in regular contact, and I continue to help where I can, whether that's guiding course selection, pointing them toward opportunities that fit their interests, or sharing what worked for me as an international graduate student.

September 2025

2nd Place Poster Award – IOE Research Symposium 2025

University of Michigan, Ann Arbor

Our team, the Reducing Emergency Department Crowding (REDC) group, earned 2nd place out of 16 undergraduate research projects at the 2025 IOE Undergraduate Research Symposium for the poster "Optimizing CT Scan Operations in the Michigan Medicine Emergency Department." The team consisted of Sandra Hawil, Laney Hoving, Grace Phan, Nethra Vijayakumar, Aimeng Yang, and myself, working under the guidance of Professor Amy Cohn and Vincenzo Loffredo.

We presented our work to IOE Department Chair Julie Simmons Ivy and to prospective students attending the undergraduate workshop, walking them through both the technical approach and its operational impact on Michigan Medicine. The Q&A session drew active interest from the Chair and faculty, sparking follow-up conversations about extending the framework to other ED bottlenecks. Our recommendations were shared with Michigan Medicine leadership as part of ongoing CHEPS engagement with the ED, and the project has since been expanded to examine additional bottlenecks across the department.

Recognized for Excellence in Election Duty – Mock Polls and EVM Commissioning

Election Commission of India – Punjab Legislative Assembly Election Duty

In the 2022 Punjab Legislative Assembly election, I participated in election duty in Bassi Pathana, Punjab, India. I facilitated the commissioning and setup of Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs), conducted mock polls, and coordinated with a team of officials to manage the voting process. It was a demanding responsibility, and I received an excellent rating from my Election Incharge for the way I carried out my duties during the election.
